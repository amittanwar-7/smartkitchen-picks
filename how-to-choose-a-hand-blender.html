How to Choose a Hand Blender for an Indian Kitchen

By SmartKitchen Picks India · Last updated 2026-10-27

A hand blender, also called an immersion blender, is compact and handy for soups, lassi, batters and smoothies. It is not a replacement for a mixer-grinder, so it is worth knowing what it can do.

Use this checklist before you buy.

What a hand blender is good at

  • Blending soups and dal directly in the pot.
  • Making lassi, milkshakes and smoothies in a tall jug.
  • Smoothing sauces, chutneys with soft ingredients and pancake batter.
  • Whisking, if it has a whisk attachment.

What it is not good at

  • Grinding dry masalas.
  • Making thick idli or dosa batter in bulk.
  • Crushing a lot of ice, unless the listing says it can.

Specs to check

  • Motor power: roughly 200 to 400 watts is common for home use. Higher watts handle thicker mixtures.
  • Blade shaft: stainless steel resists staining from turmeric. Plastic shafts can discolour.
  • Speed control: variable or at least two speeds gives better control.
  • Attachments: a jug, whisk or chopper bowl are useful extras, not essentials.
  • Warranty and service: check what is covered and how to claim.

Using it safely

Do not run it in the air, and do not lift it out of hot liquid while the blade is spinning. Let very hot soup cool slightly before blending to avoid splashes. Unplug before changing or washing attachments.

Frequently asked questions

Is a hand blender enough if I have a mixer grinder?

They do different jobs. A hand blender is for soft and liquid mixtures, and a mixer grinder is for dry grinding and thick batters.

What wattage is enough?

Around 200 to 400 watts suits everyday soups and lassi. Choose higher if you blend thick mixtures often.

Can I wash the whole hand blender?

No. Only detach the shaft to wash. Keep the motor body dry.

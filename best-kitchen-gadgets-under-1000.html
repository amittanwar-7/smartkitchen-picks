How to choose small appliances and cookware

  • Check the warranty and who handles it. A brand with service centres in your city is worth paying a little more for.
  • Look at the power rating in watts and the capacity in litres or grams, and match them to how many people you cook for.
  • For anything electric, look for an ISI mark or another recognised safety mark and a three-pin plug that fits your sockets.
  • Check whether replacement blades, lids or jars are sold separately. If not, one broken part ends the product's life.
  • Measure your counter or storage space against the product dimensions in the listing.

Induction and gas compatibility

If you use an induction cooktop, cookware needs a magnetic base. Listings for tawas and pans usually say 'induction base' or 'induction compatible'. A simple test at home is to see whether a fridge magnet sticks firmly to the bottom of the pan.

Most induction-compatible cookware also works on gas, but not every gas-only product works on induction, so check the listing before you buy.

2. Hand blender

A hand blender purees soups, blends lassi and smoothies, and mixes batter directly in the pot or jug, using much less space than a full mixer.

Best for

Small kitchens and people who already own a mixer-grinder for heavy work.

What to look for

  • Motor power around 200 to 400 watts for everyday use.
  • A stainless steel blending shaft rather than a plastic one, which can stain with turmeric.
  • A detachable shaft so you can wash it easily.

What to avoid

  • Models with no warranty or service information.
  • Using it to grind dry spices or hard ingredients, which it is not designed to do.

3. Digital kitchen scale

A scale makes baking exact and helps with portion control and diet tracking.

Best for

Bakers, fitness-minded cooks and anyone following recipes by weight.

What to look for

4. Non-stick tawa

A good non-stick tawa makes dosa, chilla and parathas with little oil and is easier to clean than plain iron.

Best for

Daily roti, dosa and paratha makers.

What to look for

  • A riveted handle that stays attached, and an induction base if you need it.

What to avoid

  • Using metal spatulas, which scratch the coating. Use wooden or silicone tools.
  • Heating an empty pan for long periods, which damages the coating.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a small appliance without checking its wattage, then finding it cannot handle your recipes.
  • Skipping the warranty details. Many problems show up in the first six months.
  • Choosing cookware without checking whether it works on your stove type.
  • Buying a gadget for a recipe you will cook once. Buy for what you cook every week.

Frequently asked questions

Is a hand blender enough, or do I need a mixer grinder?

A hand blender handles soups, lassi, batter and smoothies. A mixer grinder is better for grinding masalas, chutneys and idli batter in bulk. Many homes use both.

How can I check if a tawa works on induction?

Check for the words 'induction base' in the listing, and test at home with a magnet. If the magnet sticks firmly to the base, it will work on induction.

Is a non-stick coating safe?

Modern non-stick cookware that states PFOA-free is considered safe for normal use. Avoid overheating it empty, avoid metal utensils, and replace the pan if the coating peels.

How long should a kitchen appliance last?

Basic appliances commonly last a few years with careful use, but it depends on the brand, usage and how they are cleaned. Warranty and service support are a better guide than price.

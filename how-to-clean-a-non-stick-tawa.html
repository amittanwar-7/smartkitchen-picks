How to Clean and Care for a Non-Stick Tawa So It Lasts

A non-stick tawa can last for years or die in months, and the difference is almost always how it is used and cleaned. The coating is a thin layer that does not tolerate scratching or overheating.

These simple habits keep the surface working. They also tell you when a pan is no longer safe to use.

Daily cleaning

  • Let the tawa cool before washing. Plunging a hot pan into cold water can warp it and damage the coating.
  • Wash with warm water, mild dish soap and a soft sponge or cloth.
  • Do not use steel wool, a rough scourer or harsh powders.
  • Dry it fully before putting it away.

Removing stuck-on residue

For burnt-on spots, soak the tawa in warm soapy water for 15 to 20 minutes and wipe gently. A paste of baking soda and water applied with a soft cloth lifts stains without scratching. Do not scrape with a knife.

Utensils and heat

  • Use wooden, silicone or nylon spatulas. Metal tools scratch the coating.
  • Do not heat an empty non-stick pan on high for a long time. Overheating damages the coating and is the most common cause of failure.
  • Cook on low to medium heat. Non-stick is meant to need less oil and less heat.
  • Avoid aerosol cooking sprays, which can build up a sticky residue.

When to replace it

Replace the tawa if the coating is peeling, flaking or deeply scratched, if food sticks even with oil or if the base has warped and no longer sits flat. A worn coating is not worth continuing with.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wash a non-stick tawa in a dishwasher?

Check the maker's instructions. Hand washing is gentler and usually makes the coating last longer.

Why does my roti stick to a non-stick tawa?

Residue from earlier cooking or a worn coating is the usual cause. Clean it well and check for scratches.

Is a scratched non-stick tawa safe?

Light surface marks are common, but if the coating is flaking, replace the pan.

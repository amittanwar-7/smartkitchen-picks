How to Store Dal, Rice and Flour in the Monsoon

By SmartKitchen Picks India · Last updated 2026-10-27

Humidity during the monsoon makes grains clump, flour go stale and snacks turn soft. Weevils and mould follow. Most of it can be prevented with dry storage and the right containers.

These steps are simple and low cost.

Start dry

  • Only store grains and pulses that are completely dry. Spread them on a clean sheet for a short while before filling a container if they feel damp.
  • Wash and fully dry every container before refilling. Trapped moisture is the main cause of mould.

Choose the right containers

  • Airtight lids with silicone seals keep out humid air.
  • Containers that are the right size. Large containers with a little grain inside hold a lot of air and moisture.
  • Stainless steel and BPA-free plastic or glass all work. Avoid containers with cracked lids.

Keep pests away

  • Store containers off the floor and away from damp walls.
  • Do not mix new stock with old. Finish the old stock first.
  • Check stored items regularly for webbing, small insects or an off smell.

Flour, snacks and spices

Atta and besan go stale fastest, so buy in amounts you finish within a month or two. Keep crisp snacks in airtight boxes, and put a small clean cloth packet of dry rice at the bottom of a spice jar if humidity is a problem.

Frequently asked questions

Do bay leaves or neem leaves keep insects away?

Many households use them traditionally. They may help, but dry storage and airtight containers matter more.

How long does rice last in a container?

Properly dried and stored rice lasts a long time, but check for smell and insects regularly. Use older stock first.

Should I refrigerate atta in the monsoon?

Some people do to extend its life. If you do, use an airtight container and bring it to room temperature before opening to avoid condensation.

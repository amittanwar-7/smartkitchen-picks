Steel vs Plastic Lunch Box: Which Is Better for School and Office?

Choosing between a steel and a plastic lunch box comes down to who carries it, where it is eaten and whether it needs to be heated. Neither is best for everyone.

This guide compares them on the points that matter in daily use.

Stainless steel lunch boxes

  • Pros: durable, does not stain or hold smells, no plastic contact with hot food, easy to clean.
  • Cons: heavier, cannot go in a microwave, and can dent if dropped.
  • Best for: office use, adults and anyone who wants a box that lasts years.

Plastic lunch boxes

  • Pros: light, usually cheaper, many are microwave-safe, and they come in child-friendly shapes.
  • Cons: can stain from turmeric and tomato gravy, may hold smells and wear out sooner.
  • Best for: school children and anyone who reheats food in a microwave.

What to check on either type

  • Material information in the listing. Steel should state the grade and plastic should say BPA-free and food-grade.
  • A working seal. Test with water before packing curry.
  • Microwave and dishwasher ratings, if you plan to use them.
  • Separate compartments if you carry roti and sabzi together.

Our simple rule

Choose steel for adults and daily office use. Choose lighter, rounded plastic for young children, and reserve it for food you do not need to keep hot for hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to put hot food in a plastic lunch box?

Use boxes labelled food-grade and BPA-free, and let very hot food cool slightly first. Check the maker's temperature guidance.

Can I microwave a steel lunch box?

No. Metal should not go in a microwave. Move food to a microwave-safe dish first.

Which keeps food warmer, steel or plastic?

Neither is insulated by default. Look for an insulated model if you want food to stay warm for hours.
